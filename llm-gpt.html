01Language models語言模型

  • 1970–80s: rule-based NLP by hand. 1990s: statistical inference of "validity" rules from large document collections.
  • A language model is a probabilistic model of word order: P("I love you") high, P("eat pen north") low. It picks up spelling (P("chulling") = 0), grammar ("one water" = 0, "two books"), and structure (subject–verb–object) from statistics.
  • It also learns style: train on all of Shakespeare and it writes Shakespeare; train on a CS textbook plus the Bible and you get a mix of both.
P(S) = P(w₁w₂…wₙ) = P(w₁) · P(w₂|w₁) · P(w₃|w₁w₂) · … · P(wₙ|w₁…wₙ₋₁) Generation: next word ~ P(wₙ | w₁…wₙ₋₁)

Before GPT, models like BERT were pre-trained then fine-tuned with supervised learning for each task. Limits: needs lots of labelled data per task, and doesn't generalise to other tasks.

02The GPT familyGPT 系列

BERT vs GPT

  • GPT uses the decoder only → unidirectional, generates text.
  • BERT uses the encoder → bidirectional, builds representations.
  • Size: BERT-base 110M, BERT-large 340M; GPT-1 117M → GPT-2 1.5B → GPT-3 175B.
  • BERT and GPT-1 need task-specific fine-tuning; later GPTs are reprogrammed by prompts and few-shot examples.
  • Keys to success: data (predict the next word well) and compute (scale up).

What GPT does

"Generative Pre-trained Transformer": compresses world knowledge through language modelling with a decoder-only transformer, memorises semantics of that knowledge, and becomes a general-purpose task solver.

Scaling laws (DeepMind 2022)

Three ingredients: model scale N (params, layers, embedding size), dataset size D, compute budget C. Performance improves smoothly as each grows: a power law, loss ∝ (1/X)^α for X = N, D, C. Most LLMs are under-trained.

Emergent abilities (Google 2022)

An ability is emergent if it is absent in smaller models but present in larger ones. It's the most prominent feature of LLMs and raises "the larger the better?"

03Generating the next word生成下一個字

"Russia has declared war on the United States after Donald Trump accidentally fired…": the model embeds "Trump", attends to "Donald", "Russia", "declared war", and outputs a probability for every word (pressed 2.35%, fired 2.3%, …). The pick depends on attention and temperature. Longer context (GPT-3: 2048 tokens) gives more precise answers; early ChatGPT forgot long conversations.

Goal (translation X → Y): Y_best = argmax_Y P(Y | X) Two kinds of error: the most probable output is bad → fix the MODEL the search misses the most probable output → fix the SEARCH Exhaustive search: Bˢ sentences (B branches, s = sentence length) → far too expensive
Greedy vs beam search (lecture tree)

First word: the 0.24, I 0.20, give 0.10, …

Greedy takes "the" (highest). Beam (k = 3) keeps the, I, give.

Second word. After "the": nice 0.1, cat 0.3, dog 0.2. After "I": am 0.25, sit 0.05, love 0.3.

the cat = 0.24 × 0.3 = 0.072 ← greedy path the dog = 0.24 × 0.2 = 0.048 I love = 0.20 × 0.3 = 0.060 I am = 0.20 × 0.25 = 0.050 the nice = 0.024 Beam keeps the top 3: the cat, I love, I am (or the dog)

Third word. After "the cat": runs 0.3, wait 0.1, barks 0.05. After "I love": you 0.4.

Greedy: the cat runs = 0.072 × 0.3 = 0.0216 Beam: I love you = 0.060 × 0.4 = 0.0240 ← higher!

Greedy missed the best sentence because "I" was not the most likely first word. That is problem 1 of greedy decoding; problem 2 is that it prefers several common words over one rare word.

Beam search finishes each hypothesis at <eos>, shrinks the beam as hypotheses end, then compares complete sentences using a length-normalised score (longer sentences naturally have lower probability).

Greedy search 貪婪搜尋

y_j = argmax P(y_j | X, y₁…y_{j−1}). Fast, but can miss Y_best and prefers common words.

Beam search 集束搜尋

Breadth-first: keep the top k (beam size) sequences at each step. Deterministic, good for accuracy (translation, summarisation), but low diversity and higher cost.

04Sampling, temperature and the long tail抽樣與溫度

Sampling y_j ~ P(y_j | X, y₁…y_{j−1}) adds diversity, but the long tail is a problem: each unlikely token has a tiny probability, yet together they add up, so sampling sometimes picks a wrong one. That leads to hallucinations. Fixes cut the tail:

05Perplexity and watermarking困惑度與浮水印

To compare sentences of different lengths, normalise the probability with the geometric mean (because sentence probability is a product).

p_norm(x₁…xₙ) = p(x₁…xₙ)^(1/n) higher is better Perplexity PPL = 1 / p_norm = p(x₁…xₙ)^(−1/n) LOWER is better e.g. word probabilities 0.5, 0.25, 0.2, 0.4 : p = 0.01, p_norm = 0.01^(1/4) = 0.316, PPL = 3.16
Perplexity calculatortry it

Watermarking (Claude, Aug 2026 per slides): random sampling uses a random-number generator; set its seed from a secret key plus the preceding tokens. Output quality is unchanged, but anyone with the key can re-run the choices and detect the pattern. Without the key it can't be detected, and editing words can remove it.

06From baseline model to useful assistant由基礎模型到助手

  • A baseline LLM is a probability machine: trained with loss ℒ = −log p_y to predict the next token from statistical patterns, not meaning. Coherent but "mostly useless": no reasoning, truthfulness or intent; it diverges and hallucinates. "LLMs are not intelligent, just statistical beasts."
  • Three steps, as for any ML model: (1) collect massive data, (2) pre-train a baseline (autocomplete), (3) fine-tune to make it useful (knowledge, factual consistency, human alignment). Fine-tuning needs a large human-labelled dataset; annotation is slow.
  • GPT-3's problems (not aligned with user intent): unhelpful (ignores instructions), hallucinations, poor interpretability, toxic or biased content.

RLHF incorporates human expertise, reduces untruthful and toxic output and hallucination, and aligns the model with users. It is labour-intensive.

07ChatGPTChatGPT

How it was built

  • Based on GPT-3 (175B, 96 layers, 96 heads, context 2048) and InstructGPT. Slides give ChatGPT itself as ~20B params, embedding 1536, input size 8192.
  • Conversation-protocol fine-tuning: special tokens like <|im_start|>, <|im_end|> mark turns ("User: … Assistant: …"). Plain next-word prediction would just continue your text.
  • Steps: (1) InstructGPT with RLHF; (2) human trainers write dialogues using model suggestions; (3) mix that dialogue data with InstructGPT data.
  • 1M users in 5 days; 100M monthly users in 2 months (TikTok took 9).

Embedding + decoder

Input embedding learns the meaning and relationships of the input words (large embedding, many heads). The decoder generates fluent text (needs a large model, lots of data and a good input embedding).

Helpfulness vs harmlessness

Push helpfulness → more harmful outputs. Push harmlessness → refuses more, less helpful. Needs a balance.

#ChatGPT limitations (slides)
1Plausible but wrong answers (hallucination): RL has no source of truth; more caution declines correct answers; trained on available data, not what humans know.
2Sensitive to rephrasing or repeating the same prompt.
3Verbose, overuses phrases (trainers preferred longer answers).
4Guesses intent instead of asking for clarification.
5May follow harmful instructions or show bias.

Data privacy: Jasper AI built a copywriting service on OpenAI's API. Prompts sent to the API were recorded; ChatGPT got better at emails and essays, and users switched to ChatGPT. Lesson: what you send to an LLM API can be used.
